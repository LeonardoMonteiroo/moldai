# Seams and assembly

> This is the _why_. For the operative rule, see [`../architecture/seams-and-assembly.md`](../architecture/seams-and-assembly.md).
> For universal, cross-cutting rules, see the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md).

### Seam-conflict detection (live, apply-with-warning) — the seam graph (#134)

- **`draftAll` (`lib/draft/garment.ts`) builds a real seam graph, not a single hardcoded comparison.** Each drafter declares its own edge-index ranges on `DraftedPiece.seamEdges` (`lib/draft/core.ts` — `{shoulder, side, armhole, hem}` from `body.ts`'s `bodice()`, `{cap, underarmRight, underarmLeft}` from `sleeve.ts`, `{waist}` from `skirt.ts`), and `draftAll` — the one place that knows the whole garment's topology, i.e. which panel sews to which — pairs matching edges across pieces into `SeamPairDecl` entries (`lib/draft/seams.ts`): front vs back shoulder, front vs back side seam, front+back armhole vs the sleeve cap (with the cap's own `CAP_EASE_CM` as the pair's intended ease), the sleeve's own two underarm edges, and — for a dress — each of the bodice's hem edges vs the skirt panel's waist edge. `walkSeamPairs` (`lib/draft/seams.ts`) is the pure function that measures every declared pair's real arc length off the already-drafted pieces (`curveArcLengthCm`, never a bespoke per-drafter number) and returns the full graph — every pair, divergent or not. This is the data structure issue #138 (assembly/sewing-order instructions) walks.
- **`seamCheck` (`lib/draft/garment.ts`) is a consumer of that graph, not the comparison itself** — it narrows the full list down to the pairs whose actual gap misses their own intended ease by more than `SEAM_TOLERANCE_CM` (1 cm, unchanged from before this issue). `applyGarmentSpec` calls it on `draftAll`'s `seamGraph` and `detectConflict` (`lib/editor/state.ts`) attaches a real `ConflictInfo` when anything comes back. The generation still applies, bumps the version, and debits; the chat message gets `tone: "conflict"` and the route responds `status: "conflict"`.
- **Which pairs are genuinely at risk vs. equal by construction today:** the armhole/cap pair is the one divergence possible before this issue — `style.armholeDeepenCm` (0–6 cm, AI-settable, `validateSpec`-clamped) deepens only the body's armhole while the cap is sized from the base one, unless refit. Shoulder, side-seam and the sleeve's own underarm pair are equal by construction (neither side of any of those three depends on anything that currently varies independently between the two pieces involved) — declared and checked anyway, as regression guards for #138's own graph and for whatever a future drafter change might unbalance, not because they can diverge today. **The dress waist-join pair was a REAL, previously-undetected gap #134 surfaced, closed by #177 — in two passes, because the first pass's own fix (#185) was itself incomplete.** For a straight/a-line skirt it was already equal by construction (`skirtWaistHalfCm`, #79, shared by both sides), but for a circle/gore/half-circle skirt the bodice hem was still hip-derived (`skirtWaistHalfCm(hip)`) while the skirt's own waist arc was drafted straight from the actual waist measurement — two different measurements of the same body, genuinely divergent for most real proportions (measured at `SIZE_TABLE.M`: 54 cm hip-derived hem vs. 37 cm circle/gore panel arc, 74 cm half-circle panel arc — 17–20 cm off, not a rounding-scale gap). **#177's decision: fix the bodice, not the skirt** — a fitted bodice ends at the natural waist, so sizing its hem off the hip was wrong independent of silhouette; the circle skirt only made it visible. The value both sides now read is `dressWaistHalfCm(waistCm)` (`lib/draft/skirt.ts`), and it takes **no silhouette parameter at all** — the bodice describes the body, so which skirt happens to be attached cannot change its own hem width. It resolves to `waistCm / 4`, with no added design ease: not an arbitrary pick, but the one value every silhouette's own construction can meet exactly — a circle/gore panel's own arc is `waistCm/2` (two quarters, one per bodice half) and a half-circle panel's single arc is the full `waistCm` (split between the two halves, see below) — so front + back always sum to exactly `waistCm`. A standalone skirt's own waist ease (`skirtWaistHalfCm`, `SKIRT_WEARING_EASE_CM`) is deliberately not reused here: that ease is for a separately-worn waistband, not a dress's already-eased-bodice waist seam. **The topology also has to match the seam's own physical shape, which #177's first pass (measured, not merely reasoned about) got wrong for half-circle**: a first cut of this fix made both sides' _value_ equal for all five silhouettes, verified by the seam graph reporting zero divergence — but for half-circle specifically, the skirt is a single undivided panel (`qty: 1`) whose one waist arc already equals the _full_ waist measurement, and comparing both the bodice front hem and back hem _individually_ against that same whole arc (the topology `waist-join-front`/`waist-join-back` had used for every silhouette since #134) meant satisfying "zero divergence" required drafting each bodice half to the _entire_ waist circumference — a front hem of 74 cm and a back hem of 74 cm at `SIZE_TABLE.M`, a bodice roughly twice the width of the body it's supposed to fit. The seam graph reported the fix as correct because the check itself was asking the wrong question, not because the garment was right — filed as #185 and closed by folding its fix into this same PR rather than shipping the first pass. The real fix: `skirtPiece.qty === 1` (today, only ever half-circle) is the general signal that a skirt is one undivided panel rather than two identical cut copies — `draftSkirt` (`lib/draft/skirt.ts`) exposes `waistFront`/`waistBack`, the panel's one waist arc split at its own centre vertex (the same vertex `foldEdge` already anchors on), and `garment.ts`'s `draftAll` pairs each bodice half against its own half of the arc instead of the whole thing whenever `qty === 1`; for `qty > 1` (straight/a-line, circle/gore) each cut copy is already a whole panel matching one bodice half entirely, unchanged from #79's original topology. Verified live: all five silhouettes now report the _same_ 37 cm bodice-hem value (front and back), summing to the full 74 cm waist, `diffCm: 0` on both pairs. **Every dress's geometry changed as a result of #177's underlying value move, including straight/a-line dresses that already closed by construction**: they were sized off the _hip_ (`skirtWaistHalfCm`), which a fitted bodice hem never should have read regardless of whether it happened to agree with the skirt panel; a dress persisted before #177 re-drafts with the new (smaller, waist-derived, not hip-derived) hem/waist width the next time it's regenerated, since a pattern re-drafts from its frozen `sizeTable` on every regeneration and nothing about the snapshot itself changed. **A standalone skirt is untouched** — `skirtWaistHalfCm(hip)` still sizes its own straight/a-line panel, and a standalone half-circle/circle/gore skirt's own geometry (`radialSkirt`) is untouched too; only a dress's waist join (both the value and, for half-circle, the topology) moved. **#129/#130/#131 added three more pairs to the graph** (`collar-neckline`, `cuff-hem`, `waistband-waist`, see Drafting engine below) — all three equal by construction like shoulder/side/sleeve-underarm above, since each sub-piece's own seam is sized directly from the edge it mates to. **#128 added five more, for the new `trousers` archetype** (`trouser-front-rise`, `trouser-back-rise`, `trouser-inseam`, `trouser-outseam`, `trouser-waistband-waist`, see Drafting engine below) — also all equal by construction, the same shape as the sub-piece pairs above.
- **Multiple simultaneous divergences render as a list, not multiple panels.** `ConflictInfo.entries?: ConflictEntry[]` (additive; absent on a conflict persisted before this issue, which then still renders exactly as it always did through the singular `title`/`description`/`diffCm`/`pieceAId`/`pieceBId`/`markers` fields, populated from the primary entry — the armhole/cap one when present, else the first found) holds one entry per divergence: a pre-resolved `label`/`aLabel`/`bLabel` (the same draft-time locale snapshot `title`/`suggestion` already are), `diffCm`, `fixable`, and its own canvas `markers`. The canvas's single top-right overlay slot still holds exactly one card (`ConflictInfoCard`), which now renders one label+two-bar group per entry instead of always exactly one; `PatternCanvas` draws one marker pair per entry. **The two divergent lengths behind each entry are NOT persisted** — `ConflictEntry` carries only `diffCm`, matching the pre-#134 rule (`ConflictInfo` persisted only `diffCm`) that a pile of per-seam numbers in the blob would have broken. `measureSeamGraph(spec, baseMeasurements, t)` (`lib/draft/garment.ts` — `draftAll`'s `seamGraph` without the pieces or the layout, the `measureSeams` precedent generalised) is called once in `CanvasArea` and its results are matched back to each entry by `key`, the same "derive at render time" rule `fitPieceLabel`/`measureSeams` already established — and it's what gives the comparison to conflicts persisted before this issue too. The bare-`entries` fallback and the fully-legacy (no `entries` at all) fallback both still exist in `ConflictInfoCard`/`PatternCanvas` for exactly that reason.
- `POST /api/patterns/[id]/conflict/resolve`: "fix" calls `refitSleeve` for free — it sets the internal `style.sleeveRefit` and re-drafts so the cap uses the adjusted armhole, closing only the armhole/cap divergence (any other entry in the list is untouched, and the conflict stays open with whatever remains); "ignore" clears the whole warning regardless of how many entries it holds, leaving every pair divergent. `sleeveRefit` is deliberately **not** accepted by `validateSpec`, so the AI can never fake a refit. **`ConflictActions`'s "Corrigir manga" button itself is conditional now**: it renders only while at least one entry is `fixable` (today, only ever the armhole/cap pair) — a conflict made up entirely of unfixable divergences shows the two lengths with no fix button to offer, per this issue's own decision, rather than a button that would silently do nothing for what's actually wrong. (The dress-waist-join pair was the one live example of this; #177 closed it, so today it's a regression guard, not a case a user can actually hit.)

### Assembly / sewing order (#138)

- **The sewing order is derived data, walked from the seam graph — not a hand-written sequence per garment archetype.** `lib/draft/assembly.ts`'s `buildAssemblyOrder(pattern, t)` (the `buildEaseReport`/`measureSeamGraph` precedent: pure, DB-free, redrafts via `draftPiecesCm`/`measureSeamGraph` off the pattern's own frozen `sizeTable`, so it works on a pattern drafted before this issue existed and is never persisted) produces one ordered `steps: AssemblyStep[]` list: a `"dart"` step per piece that has any (from `dartsOf(piece.internalsCm)`), one `"seam"` step per `SeamMeasurement` the graph actually walked (never more, never fewer — the AC's own invariant, `assembly.test.ts`'s matrix suite), and a `"hem"` step for any piece whose own `seamEdges.hem` edge isn't already claimed by another declared pair (see `HEM_CONSUMED_BY` below).
- **Ordering among the seam steps is a lookup by the pair's own stable `key`, not a branch on `spec.type`.** `PHASE_BY_KEY` (`lib/draft/assembly.ts`, exported for `assembly.test.ts`'s own exhaustiveness guard) maps every pair key to one of six construction phases — `primary` (shoulder; a trouser leg's own rise, sewn to its own mirrored copy), `sleeve` (cap-into-armhole and the sleeve's own underarm), `side` (side seam; trouser inseam/outseam), `attach` (collar, cuff), `waistJoin` (a dress bodice sewn to its skirt panel), `band` (waistband, last of the seams) — and darts are unconditionally sewn before every seam/hem step (a piece's darts shape it before any seam joins it to another piece; putting every piece's darts ahead of every seam, rather than interleaving per-piece, is a stricter ordering, not a looser one). A future pair key with no entry falls to `"other"` (sewn last among seams, never dropped) rather than crashing — but every key the drafting matrix actually produces today is exhaustively covered, asserted directly.
- **Each seam step cites the two real, already-drafted lengths (`aLenCm`/`bLenCm`) and the pair's own intended `easeCm`** — `SeamMeasurement` (`lib/draft/seams.ts`) now carries `easeCm` (additive: `pair.easeCm`, carried through unchanged by `walkSeamPairs`, alongside the existing `diffCm`) precisely so "how much ease to distribute" (e.g. the sleeve cap's `CAP_EASE_CM`, a waistband's `WAISTBAND_OVERLAP_CM`) is a real number a sewing-order step can cite, not a guess. `diverges` (from `seamCheck`, not a second threshold) flags a seam that is a live conflict; `AssemblyOrder.hasConflict` is true when any step does — the order is still returned (still real, still useful to know the sequence), but a consumer must say so rather than presenting a plausible-looking, fully-closing seam.
- **Piece names come from the pattern's OWN already-persisted, already-locale-resolved `pieces` list (`pieceDisplayName`, matched by id), never from a fresh redraft.** The same draft-time-locale-snapshot rule every other piece of persisted display text follows (i18n, below) — it's also what keeps a step's `pieceName` byte-identical to what `TechSheetTab`/the canvas already show for the same piece. A seam step's own `key` (e.g. `"armhole-cap"`, `"waist-join-front"`) is deliberately **not** resolved through a catalog label here — it's handed to the model as a stable English identifier for it to phrase itself, the same "hand the model a semantic English label and let it translate/phrase it" precedent the prompt's own `COST_DESCRIPTIONS`/`EXPORT_FORMATS` already use, rather than adding a second Translate-threading path into a module that otherwise needs none.
- **A piece set with no declared pairs, no darts, and no free hem edge derives an honestly empty order (`steps: []`), not a fabricated one** — the shape stays, for a future piece with genuinely nothing to derive, but no spec the drafting matrix produces reaches it today: a standalone skirt/trousers with `waistband: "none"` used to be that reachable example (neither archetype declared any other mated pair, and neither declared a `hem` edge either), but #188 gave both their own `seamEdges.hem` (see below), so that same spec now derives one real `"hem"` step instead. `lib/ai/prompt.ts`'s `# Assembly / sewing order` instructions still tell the model to say plainly that no order could be derived rather than inventing one, for whenever the shape is reached again.
- **Reachable from chat, not a new screen** — the recorded product preference. A sewing-order question answers through the existing `decision: "explain"` path (same `COSTS.adjust` debit, same credit chip, consistent with `explain` never being refunded because the Anthropic call is real and the user gets what they asked for), with one addition: `RawToolInput`/`AiDecision` (`lib/ai/schema.ts`/`decision.ts`) gain an `assemblyOrder?: boolean`, set by the model **only** alongside `decision: "explain"` when its answer specifically narrates the derived order — `resolveDecision` strips it for every other decision, even if the model set it on an `apply`. The route (`app/api/patterns/[id]/generate`) tags the persisted `ChatMessage` with `assembly: true` when that flag comes back, and `ChatBubble` renders the catalog-sourced `ChatBubble.assemblyDisclaimer` note under the bubble when it's set — **"the surrounding chrome and the disclaimer come from the catalog," not the model** (the step text itself is model-produced prose, outside the catalog, exactly like `assistantMessage` always is), satisfying "labelled as AI-generated guidance, not verified construction advice" as a structural signal rather than the route sniffing the model's own prose for keywords.
- **The model may never invent a step for a seam absent from the structure** — enforced by instruction, not by the route re-validating the model's prose against the JSON (the same trust level every other `explain` answer already has for the ease report/product facts it's handed). `buildSystem` (`lib/ai/prompt.ts`) embeds `buildAssemblyOrder`'s JSON under "Assembly order" whenever one can be derived, and its own `# Assembly / sewing order` section is the one place that instructs: walk the steps in order, name pieces by `aPieceName`/`bPieceName`, cite lengths and (only when meaningfully above 0) the ease to distribute, and state when a step `diverges` that the seam doesn't currently close and needs the conflict resolved first.
- **#188: a skirt panel's own bottom hem (every silhouette) and a trouser leg's own hem now declare `seamEdges.hem` too**, alongside body.ts's/sleeve.ts's pre-existing ones — `lib/draft/skirt.ts`'s straight/a-line panel exposes the straight edge through its own fold-bottom vertex, and its circle/gore/half-circle panels expose the already-curved `hemSpan` used to build the outline; `lib/draft/trousers.ts`'s leg panel exposes the straight edge between its own named `hemInner`/`hemOuter` vertices. Neither is ever claimed by another pair — at the time this shipped, `HEM_CONSUMED_BY` (`lib/draft/assembly.ts`) only knew about `body-front`/`body-back` (consumed by `waist-join-front`/`-back` on a dress) and `sleeve` (consumed by `cuff-hem`), because a skirt/trouser leg's own hem, unlike its waist, is never sewn to anything (not a bodice at a dress's waist join, not a waistband) — so both now always produce a real `"hem"` step once the piece is drafted, standalone or as part of a dress. `HEM_CONSUMED_BY` itself is gone as of #323, below — the fact it recorded is now derived, not looked up.

### Freesewing seam keys registered in the phase table (#323)

- **`PHASE_BY_KEY` was moldai-only in practice, though nothing about its shape said so.** `#267`/`#269` registered freesewing as a second drafting engine (see Drafting engine, below), and its three archetype-only designs — hoodie (Hugo), coat (Jett), shirt (Simon) — declare 25 pair keys across their own `seamPairs` arrays that had no entry in the table at all. `phaseRank`'s `"other"` fallback (last position, no tie-break) meant those 25 keys sewed in whatever order each wrapper's own array literal happened to list them — arbitrary, not construction-derived, for the three garment types only freesewing can draft. Silent by design (the fallback exists precisely so a missing key can't crash a generation), which is exactly why it went unnoticed: `:19`'s "every key the drafting matrix actually produces today is exhaustively covered, asserted directly" was true only of `lib/draft/assembly.test.ts`'s own guard, which hardcoded `DEFAULT_ENGINE_ID` and walked `DECORATION_CASES` — a fixture matrix built before freesewing existed and never given a hoodie/coat/shirt case. Two independent gaps, neither an accident of a single line: the guard needed to walk `ENGINE_IDS`, and it needed a way to reach a type `DECORATION_CASES` has no case for.
- **The per-key phase is real construction-order judgment, not a mechanical carry-over of moldai's own six-phase vocabulary onto a different design.** The decisive case: hoodie is a raglan pattern (`Hugo`) with **no shoulder seam at all** — `hoodie-raglan-front`/`hoodie-raglan-back` are the garment's first structural closure between body and sleeve, so they take moldai's `shoulder` phase (`primary`), not `sleeve` (which would place them after a phase this design never reaches). The same reasoning applies to `coat-center-back` (Jett's back-panel center seam, a self-pair the same shape as `trouser-front-rise`/`-back-rise` already are) and `shirt-yoke-back` (Simon's yoke-to-back seam, the shirt's own first closure before either front panel is set) — both `primary`, both closed before any cross-panel join. `hoodie-hood-center` is also `primary`: a component closed on itself (the hood's own 3-panel seam) before it could be joined to anything else, on the same footing as `coat-center-back`/`shirt-yoke-back` above (**#341 later made this pair represent BOTH of the hood's two physical seams, and added the pair that actually joins the hood to the body — see below**). The two collar-attach keys on shirt (`shirt-collar-neckline`/`shirt-collar-stand-fall`) reuse moldai's own `collar-neckline`/`collar-stand-fall` two-step precedent (`#222`) exactly, in the same declared order, for the same reason: a fall sews onto the stand's own already-attached upper seam. One imperfection, left as-is rather than hidden: within `attach`, `shirt.ts` declares `shirt-cuff-wrist` _before_ `shirt-collar-neckline`, so the stable sort cites the cuff before the collar — unconventional construction order, but harmless, since neither finishing step depends on the other having happened first; fixing it would need either a seventh phase or reordering the wrapper's own array, and no sampled spec turned up a case where the two orders produce a genuinely wrong instruction, only an atypical one.
- **`HEM_CONSUMED_BY` is deleted, not extended.** The table only ever recorded a fact the seam graph already carries: `SeamMeasurement.aEdges`/`bEdges` (`#253`) hold each pair's own per-edge `{pieceId, from, to}` breakdown, and every pair declaration references a piece's `seamEdges.hem` object directly when it consumes it — so "is this piece's hem edge already sewn to something" is answerable by scanning the graph the code already has in scope, with no separate table to keep in sync. The old table was hand-maintained per moldai `pieceId` and, being untouched since before freesewing existed, was **silently wrong** for two of freesewing's own archetypes: hoodie and coat both reuse moldai's own `"body-front"`/`"body-back"` ids (their own bodies are the same conceptual panels), and both declare `seamEdges.hem` on them — but the table mapped those ids to `waist-join-front`/`waist-join-back`, pair keys that never appear in a hoodie/coat graph (their waist is closed by `hoodie-waistband-hem`/`coat-waistband-hem` instead). The result, confirmed before the fix: **two spurious "free hem" steps per hoodie/coat generation**, telling the model to hem edges the waistband pair already sews. Deriving from the graph closes this for every engine at once, present and future, rather than requiring a fourth hand-added table entry the next time a design reuses a moldai piece id.
- **The same derivation surfaced a second, moldai-only instance of the identical bug, also fixed by the same change.** The cuff piece (`lib/draft/subpieces.ts`'s `draftCuff`) declares its own `seamEdges.hem`, and that exact edge is the b-side of `cuff-hem` (`garment.ts`, and the freesewing `hoodie-cuff-wrist`/`coat-cuff-wrist`/`shirt-cuff-wrist` pairs) — sewn to the sleeve's own wrist edge. The old table had no `"cuff"` entry at all, so a cuffed sleeve always emitted a spurious extra "hem" step for the cuff on top of the real `cuff-hem` seam step, for every cuffed blouse this app has ever drafted. `assembly.test.ts`'s existing cuff regression test only ever pinned that the _sleeve's_ hem was suppressed; it never checked the cuff, so this shipped and stayed unnoticed until #323's audit derived the correct answer and compared it against the old table's output directly.
- **The exhaustiveness guard now runs over every registered engine, and is self-maintaining on both axes it previously missed.** `assembly.test.ts` walks `ENGINE_IDS` (not one hardcoded default) and, for a type a registered engine supports but `DECORATION_CASES` has no case for, sweeps a plain spec across a small `sleeve` style set (`{}`, `"short"`, `"long"`) — which is what reaches shirt's own three conditional keys (`shirt-cuff-wrist` only when `sleeve !== "short"`; the two collar keys behind their own `stand.seamEdges?.…` guards). A third engine, or a fourth freesewing-only type, needs no new fixture: it's picked up automatically the moment its own `capability.types` entry exists, the same "derive, don't hand-maintain a parallel list" reasoning behind deleting `HEM_CONSUMED_BY` above.

### The skirt panel's own side seam, and the orphan-piece gate it closes (#330)

- **A real gap, found by extending the sampled-space sampler (#256) with an "every drafted piece must appear in some declared pair" gate, not assumed in advance.** Running that gate against the existing drafting matrix found two orphans: `placket` (already known, see below) and, more consequentially, the `skirt` piece itself whenever `waistband: "none"` — a standalone skirt with no waistband declared **zero** seam pairs at all in every one of its five silhouettes. Nothing had ever sewn its own two side edges to each other; `buildAssemblyOrder` derived a `"hem"` step and nothing else, an assembly order that never once said "sew the side seams" for a garment whose two panel copies obviously need to be joined into a tube.
- **The fix is a self-pair, `skirt-side`, on the same footing as `sleeve.ts`'s pre-existing `underarmRight`/`underarmLeft` self-pair — not a new front/back topology.** `lib/draft/skirt.ts` already draws the panel with `qty: 2` (or `qty: 1` for half-circle) from ONE outline; there is no separate `pieceId` for "the front copy" vs "the back copy" the way `body-front`/`body-back` are two distinct drafted pieces. `seamEdges.sideRight`/`sideLeft` name the panel's own two straight/radial side edges (derived directly from the same vertex layout `waist`/`hem`/`foldEdge` already anchor on — the two points sharing the waist-arc's own `+half`/`-half` angle for a radial silhouette, the two straight corners for straight/a-line), and `garment.ts` declares `a: [edgeRef(skirtPiece.id, sideRight)]` vs `b: [edgeRef(skirtPiece.id, sideLeft)]` — the same piece id on both sides, exactly how `sleeve-underarm` already reads. Equal by construction (the panel is symmetric about its own fold/apex axis) on **every** silhouette, verified live: `aLenCm === bLenCm`, `diffCm: 0`, across straight/a-line/gore/circle/half-circle, with and without an active pleat (a pleat widens both sides symmetrically, so it stays equal too).
- **Declared unconditionally — dress or standalone, every silhouette, waistband or none** — not gated on `waistband !== "none"`, because the side seam exists regardless of whether a waistband is ever added on top of it. This is also why a dress's own skirt panel gets the same pair: `waist-join-front`/`waist-join-back` (above) only ever cover the WAIST edge; nothing previously declared the skirt panel's own sides on a dress either.
- **Side effect, expected and verified, not a silent behavior change**: every skirt and dress pattern's own assembly order now includes a `"side"`-phase seam step it didn't have before (`PHASE_BY_KEY["skirt-side"] = "side"`, same phase as a bodice's plain side seam) — `assembly.test.ts`'s own fixed-expectation cases were updated to include it, and the exhaustiveness guard (`#323`, above) already covers a new moldai key by construction.
- **`placket` (`lib/draft/subpieces.ts`) is the one piece this gate still tolerates as unpaired, and it stays that way** — its own doc comment states why: it binds into the sleeve's vertical opening above the cuff, and the sleeve's own drafted outline has no distinct edge there to mate against (unlike the skirt's own sides, which were always real geometry with a name just never assigned to them). Giving it a real pair would mean `sleeve.ts` drafting that opening as boundary geometry, a drafting change with its own construction-order judgment — out of scope for #330's own test-quality issue, tracked as its own follow-up instead. The sampled-space gate's own allowlist for this exception is a closed, asserted set (one entry, `placket`) rather than an open-ended exemption list — verified by emptying it and confirming the gate goes red exactly there (21/250 samples on the widest sampled body, all `placket`) before shipping it non-empty.

### The hoodie's hood, actually sewn to the body, and its neck binding drafted (#341)

- **The gap #323 found and deliberately left open: `hoodie-hood-center` closed the hood's own 3-panel construction, but nothing joined the finished hood to the body.** `buildAssemblyOrder` walks the declared seam graph only — no reverse pass asks "is this edge sewn to anything?" — so a moletom's own generated sewing order named every seam except the one that actually attaches the hood, silently, for every hoodie ever drafted. The wrapper's own comment said why: the join happens "via a neck-binding trim strip this wrapper does not draft," and `lib/draft/subpieces.ts`'s `straightBand` had been exported since #269 anticipating exactly that strip, unbuilt until now.
- **The topology was measured against Hugo's own upstream store before declaring anything, the same discipline that made Jett's own collar-to-neckline pair get written and then removed (`docs/decisions/drafting.md`, below) rather than shipped implausible.** Hugo's `front.mjs`/`back.mjs` each store `neckOpeningLenFront`/`neckOpeningLenBack` as the curve's FULL length — but the body panel's own drafted edge only carries 80% of the front curve and 70% of the back curve; the remaining 20%/30% is exactly the portion of the neck opening that runs across the sleeve's own raglan top (`sleeve.ts`'s `raglanTop`, between `raglanTipBack` and `raglanTipFront`, previously left undeclared for the identical reason). Summed correctly — front neckline (doubled, cut on fold) + back neckline (doubled) + both sleeves' own raglan-top gap — the three pieces close the full ring, verified against `2 * neckOpeningLenFront + 2 * neckOpeningLenBack` read directly from Hugo's own store, not re-derived by hand (a hand reconstruction using only the 80%/70% curve was tried first and undercounted the ring by ~11%).
- **The hood's own matching ring needed the SAME care**: `hoodSide`'s own neck edge (the arc the pre-#341 wrapper had already identified but never declared, `neckEdge` back to `frontEdge` wrapping through index 0) doubled, plus `hoodCenter`'s own short end (`hoodCenterWidthCm = head/10`) — that short end is the small connecting edge that closes the hood's neck-opening loop at center-back, and omitting it (measuring only the two `hoodSide` edges) undercounts the ring by ~11% too. With it included, the hood's ring measures a **stable ~3.3–3.7% longer than the body's own opening across every `SIZE_TABLE`/`SIZE_TABLE_MALE` row** — a real, consistent gathering ease (the hood eases onto a slightly smaller opening), the same "a real measured value, not an assumed 0" signature Jett's own `armholeCapEaseCm`/`shoulderEaseCm` established: `easeCm` on `hoodie-hood-neckline` is computed from the drafted geometry (`hoodNeckRingCm - neckRingCm`), never a fudged constant, and its cross-body stability is what stands in for a correctness check (`diffCm` is tautologically 0 once the pair's own ease is subtracted).
- **`hoodie-hood-center` now represents BOTH of the hood's two physical seams, not one.** The hood's 3-panel construction has each `hoodSide` half meeting `hoodCenter` on its own side (`hoodCenter`'s two equal-length long edges, `near`/`far` on `straightBand`'s own rectangle) — both real seams, both equal by construction (the same synthetic rectangle on both), so `a`/`b` each carry two edges now and `diffCm` stays exactly 0.
- **The neck-binding strip is drafted directly to the measured ring length, not Hugo's own upstream formula.** Hugo's live-drafted `hugo.neckBinding` part measures `2·neckOpeningLenFront + 2·neckOpeningLenBack + hoodCenterWidth` — the `+hoodCenterWidth` is upstream's own cutting-width generosity, not seam geometry, so this repo's `draftNeckBinding` sizes the strip to the ring alone (`easeCm: 0` on `hoodie-neck-binding`) and applies the seam allowance at the cut line like every other piece, the same split `draftCuff`/`draftWaistband` already use between a measured seam length and an authored band width (`NECK_BINDING_HEIGHT_CM`, not Hugo's own `length/44.2` cutting-width formula, which yields ~1 cm — useless as a band height).
- **The same six edges are named by TWO declared pairs (`hoodie-hood-neckline` and `hoodie-neck-binding`), deliberately.** The body's neck opening is genuinely sewn twice in the real garment: once to attach the hood underneath, once to apply the finishing strip on top. Nothing in the seam-graph machinery assumes an edge is claimed by at most one pair — `walkSeamPairs` has no uniqueness constraint, the orphan-piece gate (#330, above) is per-piece not per-edge, and `hemEdgeIsConsumed` only ever looks at a piece's own `seamEdges.hem` key (the binding's is `neck`) — so this is the first case that exercises deliberate edge reuse rather than the only case that happens to need it; see the operative rule recorded in `../architecture/seams-and-assembly.md`.
- **`PHASE_BY_KEY` gained two entries**: `hoodie-hood-neckline` is `attach` (a sub-piece — the hood — sewn onto an already-shaped edge, same footing as `collar-neckline`; declared before `hoodie-cuff-wrist` in `hoodie.ts`'s own array so the stable sort keeps it first among the hoodie's `attach` pairs), `hoodie-neck-binding` is `band` (a finishing strip applied over an already-closed seam, same footing as `hoodie-waistband-hem`, just at the neck instead of the hem).
